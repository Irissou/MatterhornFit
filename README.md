# Fitting the Matterhorn

Personal project (2021). I took a photo of a Matterhorn-shaped decoration on the wall of my little brother's room, extracted its skyline as a 1D profile, and fitted it with several analytic models to see which one describes the mountain's shape best.

First fit model: with a Moffat function (saved in figures/fit_moffat.png). The choice of a Moffat function is inspired by the fact that absorption and emission lines of galaxies are well modeled by these functions. As a master student in astrophysics at that time, I was fitting Gaussians and Moffats to the emission lines of the galaxies I was studying and therefore adapted

Second fit model: Moffat + polynomial fit (saved in figures/fit_moffat_poly.png). Adding a polynomial is motivated by the wiggle on the right side of the summit that makes a smooth representative fit with a single Gaussian or Moffat not possible.

## What the notebook does
1. Loads the photo and picks, for every pixel column, the darkest pixel to get the skyline.
2. Smooths the profile with a median filter and centres it on the summit.
3. Fits it with Gaussian, Moffat, skewed Gaussian/Moffat and high-degree polynomial models (`lmfit`).
4. Fits a Moffat + degree-20 polynomial model with `astropy` (top figure: Moffat only; bottom: Moffat + polynomial).
5. Draws a sun (`SUN.png`, made with Python) next to the summit for the final figures.

## Takeaway
A single Moffat profile captures the overall shape of the peak but misses its asymmetry (the right side wiggle); adding a polynomial term follows the skyline much more closely.
